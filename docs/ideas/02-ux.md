# UX Polish, Delight, and Onboarding

## Idea 1: The "Why Didn't It Fire?" Inspector

**Pitch:** A live, always-available debugger that shows the last 10 things you typed and tells you, per keystroke, exactly why a trigger did or didn't match. Text expanders fail silently and users blame themselves; Sokki would be the first to *explain itself*. This single feature eliminates 80% of frustration and support tickets.

**How it works:**
- Menu bar item: "Recent Activity" opens a translucent HUD window showing the last N captured buffers (privacy-scoped, ring buffer, never persisted).
- Each row shows the buffer typed, the candidate match (closest Levenshtein trigger), and a verdict chip: "Matched", "Too soon (needed word boundary)", "App excluded", "Secure input", "Disabled".
- Click any row to see the trigger matcher's decision trace: normalized buffer, rolling window, which prefix pruned the trie, timestamp delta to last keystroke.
- "Create match from this" button on any unmatched row — turn a typo into a snippet.
- Toggle: auto-open HUD when `isSecureInput` flips (so users instantly see why things stopped working).

**Why it's accretive:** Nothing else on the market does this. It turns the scariest moment (silent failure) into the most delightful moment (transparency + a one-click fix). Users will screenshot this and tweet it. It's also a moat: once your users learn Sokki *explains itself*, competitors feel like black boxes.

**Risk / cost:** Medium. Need a privacy-safe ring buffer with auto-wipe on focus change to password fields, and a clear "never leaves your machine" promise. Matcher must expose structured decision data, not just boolean.

## Idea 2: Ambient Expansion HUD (with a Soul)

**Pitch:** When a trigger fires, a tiny chip briefly slides up from the menu bar icon showing "`:addr` → 142 chars". Subtle, 600ms, spring-damped. Combine with a daily ambient counter in the menu bar tooltip: "37 expansions today · 4m 12s saved". You feel the app *working* instead of wondering if it's alive.

**How it works:**
- On successful expansion, post a transient `NSPanel` anchored to the status item: trigger pill on the left, arrow, truncated replacement on the right, with a hairline progress bar for the undo window.
- Hover the chip within its lifetime to *pin* it — reveals "Undo", "Edit this match", "Disable here (app-scoped)".
- Persist a rolling daily stat in UserDefaults: expansion count, estimated time saved (chars ÷ 5 ÷ avg WPM).
- Menu bar tooltip and a small stats strip at the top of the settings window surface the count; tapping "reset" is a weekly ritual.
- Respect a "Do Not Disturb" toggle and full-screen / presentation mode detection.

**Why it's accretive:** Micro-delight compounds. Users share "Sokki saved me 2.1 hours this week" screenshots — virality built-in. The pinnable chip doubles as the fastest-ever "edit this trigger" path, making matches feel malleable rather than buried in YAML.

**Risk / cost:** Low-medium. Risk of annoyance; must be genuinely subtle (opacity <0.9, no sound, respects fullscreen). Code-wise, a single `NSPanel` subclass + a tiny SwiftUI view.

## Idea 3: First-Run "Type With Me" Tutorial

**Pitch:** On first launch, instead of dumping a settings window, Sokki opens a focused 60-second interactive playground: a text field and a live caption that says "Try typing `:sokki`". When you do, the text expands and a shimmer animation celebrates. You've understood the entire product in one keystroke.

**How it works:**
- `AppDelegate` detects first run, opens a borderless onboarding window centered on screen with three panels: (1) grant Accessibility permission with a live green/red indicator polling `AXIsProcessTrusted`, (2) a sandboxed `NSTextView` with a pre-seeded `:sokki` trigger and a pulsing hint, (3) "Pick 3 starters" — email signature, current date, arrow glyphs — each with a live preview on hover.
- Each step completes only on successful demonstration (type the trigger, watch it fire in the tutorial field itself — no global capture needed for step 2).
- Finishes with "Drop a snippet here" — drag any text from any app onto the window to create a match; Sokki auto-suggests a trigger like `:siggy` from the content.
- Skippable, but rewatchable from Settings → "Replay onboarding".

**Why it's accretive:** The #1 killer of text expanders is "I installed it and forgot." First-run activation is everything. A working expansion in the first 30 seconds creates the aha moment; the drag-to-create finisher teaches a workflow that sticks.

**Risk / cost:** Medium. Accessibility permission has to be re-entrant (users deny, you recover gracefully). Step 2 needs a sandboxed matcher that doesn't require the global CGEvent tap, so users can succeed before granting permission.

## Idea 4: Spotlight-Style Trigger Palette

**Pitch:** A global hotkey (default `⌃⌥Space`) opens a Raycast-style overlay of all your matches — fuzzy-search by trigger *or* by replacement content, preview the expansion, press Return to insert at the current cursor position. Solves the "I have 200 snippets and can't remember any of them" death spiral.

**How it works:**
- Register a global `MASShortcut`-style hotkey; shows a centered floating window with a search field and a live-filtered list.
- Fuzzy match against `trigger`, `replace`, and an optional `label` field (new, optional, backward-compat).
- Right pane: rich preview of the replacement with variables resolved (`{{date}}`, `{{clipboard}}`) at the current instant.
- Return injects via the same clipboard/keystroke pipeline you already have — no new injection code needed.
- `⌘↵` on a result opens it in the editor; `⌥↵` inserts and disables for this app for the next hour.
- Recently used floats to the top; adaptive ranking per-app (`xcode` app surfaces `:todo` first).

**Why it's accretive:** Transforms Sokki from a passive background daemon into an active tool users *reach for*. This is the single feature that gets people to go from 5 triggers to 50 — because discovery stops being memory-dependent. Once a user lives in the palette, churn drops to near-zero.

**Risk / cost:** Medium-high. Needs global hotkey handling, window focus choreography (restore previous app focus before injection), and per-app usage stats. But it's reusing the entire existing injection stack — the new surface is pure UI.
